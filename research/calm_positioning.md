## How are pricing, the free trial and the subscription presented? What is free vs paid?

### Takeaway
Calm markets itself as "free to download and use" with "some of the programs and features... free forever" and no ads, while in practice the free tier is a bounded sampler (Day 1 of programs, 8 sleep sessions, a Daily Calm preview) and effectively everything flagship sits behind a padlock; the consumer plan is $14.99/month or $69.99/year, plus a web-only, final-sale Lifetime Membership.

### Cited Findings
- **CURRENT (2026-09-27)** — Google Play subscription disclosure, verbatim: "Calm is free to download and use. There are never any ads and some of the programs and features are free forever. Some content is only available through an optional paid subscription." — [Google Play](https://play.google.com/store/apps/details?id=com.calm.android&hl=en_US)
- **CURRENT (2026-09-27)** — Apple listing pricing: monthly $14.99 or annual $69.99 for unlimited access to the Calm content collection. — [Apple App Store](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/calm/id571800810)
- **CURRENT (2026-09-27)** — Calm's own help center enumerates the free tier verbatim: "230+ meditations: Guided and timed sessions for anxiety, focus, stress, and sleep"; "8 free sleep sessions: Sleep Stories and sleep audio, including the fan-favorite Blue Gold"; "Day 1 of all programs: Listen to the first session of any multi-day meditation program"; "A free Daily Calm preview: Sample Calm's flagship daily meditation series"; "The Breathe Bubble: Try this guided breathing tool anytime you need to relax"; "Background scenes: Choose from nature sounds and ambient music to play while you meditate or sleep." Premium content is marked by "a small padlock icon next to certain content." — [Calm Help Center](https://support.calm.com/hc/en-us/articles/360044707294-What-Free-Content-is-Available-on-the-Calm-App)
- **CURRENT (2026-09-27)** — Calm's help center says Premium unlocks hundreds of additional meditations, the full Sleep Story library with celebrity narrators, the daily content series, the full music catalog, guided breathing exercises, and full Calm Body/Movement access. — [Calm Help Center](https://support.calm.com/hc/en-us/articles/360044707294-What-Free-Content-is-Available-on-the-Calm-App)
- **CURRENT (2026-09-27)** — Refund policy, verbatim: "Refunds are available within 30 days of your Calm.com purchase."; "Canceling stops future renewals, but it doesn't automatically issue a refund."; "Lifetime memberships, gift cards of any length via Calm.com/Apple/Google Play, Calm Premium Family Plans, and Enterprise Plans are not eligible for a refund."; "Refunds usually appear on your original payment method within 5–10 business days." — [Calm Help Center](https://support.calm.com/hc/en-us/articles/360008240974-Calm-App-Refund-Policy-How-to-Get-Your-Money-Back)
- **CURRENT (2026-09-27)** — Lifetime Membership, verbatim: "It is a one-time payment that gives you access to Calm Premium forever."; "Lifetime Memberships are Final Sale and non-refundable."; and the warning "If you do not cancel the old subscription, the App Store will keep charging you every year." Calm states the Lifetime Membership is sold only on the website, not in-app, and that buying it does NOT cancel an existing Apple/Google recurring charge. — [Calm Help Center](https://support.calm.com/hc/en-us/articles/360012060634-Calm-Lifetime-Membership-Price-Upgrade-Steps-Cancellation-Warning)
- Calm maintains a dedicated lifetime sales page. — [calm.com/lifetime-offer](https://www.calm.com/lifetime-offer)

### Inferences
- There is a real tension between the advertised frame ("free to download and use," "free forever," "Try Calm for Free") and the gated reality that every flagship item — the celebrity Sleep Stories, the Daily Calm series proper, all programs past Day 1 — is padlocked. This is the most likely single source of "bait and switch" style review complaints, and the report can ground it entirely in Calm's own two pages.
- Calm's own documentation acknowledges a failure mode in which a user pays twice (lifetime purchase on the web while an App Store annual keeps renewing), and the lifetime purchase is simultaneously non-refundable. That combination is directly relevant to billing complaints.

### Gaps
- **The exact 2022–2023 price points could not be verified from a primary source.** Secondary aggregators reference a $69.99 annual and a lifetime tier around $399.99, but I did not verify these on a dated Calm page or archive snapshot, so the report should not state 2022–2023 prices as fact. The $14.99/$69.99 figures above are CURRENT only.
- I could not verify the exact stated length of the free trial (commonly described as 7 days) from a Calm-owned page in this research. Treat trial length as unverified.

## What emotional/outcome claims does it make, and does it cite clinical evidence?

### Takeaway
Calm makes concrete outcome claims (fall asleep faster, lower stress, anxiety relief) and backs them on the consumer site with self-reported user-survey percentages rather than trial results; its actual peer-reviewed citations live on the Calm Health B2B site, which carries an explicit disclaimer that the product is not intended to diagnose or treat any condition.

### Cited Findings
- **CURRENT (2026-09-27)** — homepage efficacy statistics, verbatim: "74% felt that Calm Kids helped lower their parenting-stress"; "63% felt that Calm Kids helped their own sleep"; "95% say Calm Kids Sleep Stories® help their child fall asleep faster"; "75% say Calm Kids meditations help their child." — [calm.com](https://www.calm.com/)

- **CURRENT (2026-09-27)** — the consumer-facing `calm.com/science` URL now 302-redirects to the Calm Health B2B site at `health.calm.com/resources/clinical-studies/`. — observed on fetch, 2026-09-27
- **CURRENT (2026-09-27)** — Calm Health's clinical studies page lists peer-reviewed citations including "Testing the Pragmatic Effectiveness of a Consumer-Based Mindfulness Mobile App in the Workplace"; "The Indirect Effects of a Mindfulness Mobile App on Productivity Through Changes in Sleep Among Retail Employees"; "Using Personalized Anchors to Establish Routine Meditation Practice with a Mobile App"; "Testing a Mindfulness Meditation Mobile App for the Treatment of Sleep-related Symptoms in Adults"; "The Effect of Meditation and Physical Activity on the Mental Health Impact of COVID-19–Related Stress"; and a featured JMIR Formative Research paper, "Leveraging a Consumer-Based Product to Develop a Cancer-Specific Mobile Meditation App." — [health.calm.com](https://health.calm.com/resources/clinical-studies/)

- **CURRENT (2026-09-27)** — Google Play expert-endorsement claim, verbatim: "Recommended by top psychologists, therapists, and mental health experts." — [Google Play](https://play.google.com/store/apps/details?id=com.calm.android&hl=en_US)

### Inferences
- The percentage statistics I could verify on the homepage are all about **Calm Kids** and are all self-reported perception ("felt that," "say"), not clinical endpoints. A report contrasting marketing claims with user complaints should be careful not to describe these as clinical efficacy evidence — Calm itself does not.
- Routing `/science` to the B2B clinical page means the consumer buyer is served survey percentages while the enterprise buyer is served journal citations. That is a defensible observation about how the evidence is segmented by audience.

- I found no verified adult-audience efficacy percentage on the current consumer homepage (e.g. a "X% report better sleep" claim for general users) — only the Calm Kids figures above. If such a stat exists elsewhere on the site I did not locate it, and none should be invented.
- Whether Calm displayed different or stronger efficacy statistics in 2022–2023 is unverified (archive block).

### Inferences
- The strongest defensible framing for the report is not "regulators acted against Calm" but "Calm's own policies structurally produce the outcomes users complain about": a 30-day, web-purchase-only refund window; a non-refundable lifetime tier; cancellation that stops renewal without refunding; and a known double-billing path between web and app-store purchases.
